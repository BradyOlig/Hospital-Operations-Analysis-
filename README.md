Hospital Operations Performance Analysis
**Project Overview**
This project analyzes hospital operational performance using staffing, patient satisfaction, admissions, capacity, and utilization data. The objective is to identify factors affecting hospital efficiency, service quality, and resource allocation, while providing data-driven recommendations for management.

**Business Problem**
Healthcare organizations must balance patient demand, staffing resources, and available capacity while maintaining high patient satisfaction and employee morale.

This analysis seeks to answer the following questions:

Which hospital services perform best?
Which departments experience the greatest capacity constraints?
Is staff attendance related to patient satisfaction?
Are certain departments experiencing excessive patient refusal rates?
What operational improvements should management consider?

**Key Findings**
Finding 1: Wait Time Is the Primary Driver of Patient Satisfaction
Analysis indicates that patient satisfaction decreases as wait times increase. Departments with shorter wait times consistently received higher satisfaction scores, suggesting that reducing delays should be a primary focus for management.

Finding 2: Emergency Department Is the Primary Capacity Constraint
The Emergency Department experiences the greatest capacity constraints, operating at 100% bed utilization while refusing more than 76% of patient requests.

Note: General Medicine also faces significant capacity pressure, operating at approximately 97% utilization. Hospital-wide utilization averages 92.7%, indicating that most departments are operating near maximum capacity. This leaves little capacity cushion to absorb increases in patient demand, likely contributing to longer wait times and higher refusal rates.

Finding 3: Staff Morale Shows Limited Impact on Patient Satisfaction
While employee morale varied between departments, the analysis found little evidence that morale was a major driver of patient satisfaction. Operational factors such as wait times and service availability appeared to have a stronger influence on patient experiences.

**Recomendation**
1. Expand Emergency Department Capacity
The Emergency Department should be evaluated for additional staffing and bed capacity to reduce refusal rates and improve patient access.

2. Improve Patient Flow Processes
Management should review admission, discharge, and transfer procedures to reduce bottlenecks and improve throughput.

3. Monitor Capacity Utilization
Departments operating above 90% utilization should be monitored closely to prevent excessive wait times and service disruptions.

4. Prioritize Wait Time Reduction
Because wait time appears to have the greatest impact on patient satisfaction, process improvement initiatives should focus on reducing patient delays.
